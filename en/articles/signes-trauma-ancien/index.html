Seven signs an old trauma is still at work today

Almost nobody comes to therapy saying “I am traumatised”. People come for wrecked sleep, anger they cannot make sense of, a tiredness that will not lift. Here is what these signs are really saying.

By Yousri Bendiabdallah, psychotherapy practitioner — trauma and emotional dependency. Morocco and online.

Untreated trauma rarely shows up where you would expect it. Almost nobody comes to see me saying “I think I am traumatised”. People come for wrecked sleep, anger they cannot make sense of, a relationship that is falling apart, a tiredness that will not lift.

Here are the seven signs I come across most often, and what they really mean. None of them is a diagnosis on its own — only a professional can make one, in a consultation. But if several of them speak to you, they deserve to be taken seriously rather than explained away with “that is just my personality”.

1. You react too strongly, and you do not understand why

A harmless remark and you are overwhelmed with anger or tears. A tone of voice, a door slamming, someone coming up behind you, and your body reacts before your mind does. Afterwards you are angry with yourself, you find it ridiculous.

This is not oversensitivity. It is an alarm system that was set for a dangerous environment and has never been updated. It goes off at resemblances, not at real dangers. The apparent disproportion is in fact proportionate — to what happened before, not to what is happening now.

2. You are exhausted for no identifiable reason

You sleep, or you think you sleep, and you wake up drained. Medical tests show nothing. You are told it is stress, and you get nothing concrete out of that.

Staying permanently on alert uses up a considerable amount of energy. A body that is constantly on watch — even without you being aware of it, even at night — does not recover. Chronic fatigue is one of the most common, and most poorly identified, traces of an old trauma.

3. You are often “somewhere else”

Moments of absence. The feeling of being behind a pane of glass, of watching yourself live. Whole journeys you have no memory of. A sense of unreality, or that your body is not quite yours.

This is dissociation. It was a remarkably effective protection: when you can neither run nor fight, you go inside. The mechanism remains available and sets itself off again, often at the worst possible moment — an argument, a moment of intimacy, an important interview.

4. You cannot bear not being in control

Anticipating everything, checking everything, never delegating, knowing the plan in advance, having a fallback for every situation. Something unexpected does not just annoy you: it fills you with anxiety.

Control is the logical response to an experience of helplessness. If you lived through something you could not prevent, controlling everything becomes the guarantee that it will not happen again. It works — at the cost of permanent exhaustion and strained relationships.

5. You feel guilty for what was done to you

“I should have left.” “I said nothing.” “I let it happen.” “I provoked it.”

This may be the most unfair sign, and the most widespread. Faced with a danger it cannot escape, the body produces a freeze response: you go still, you submit, you wait for it to be over. These are automatic biological responses, just like flight or fight. They involve no choice, and they are in no way consent. Many people carry, for decades, a guilt that does not belong to them.

6. Your relationships always follow the same script

Either you never let anyone really get close. Or you become attached immediately and completely to anyone who shows you a little attention. Often both, in turns: very close, then running away as soon as it gets serious.

You may have noticed that you always end up with “the same kind of person”. That is not bad luck. When a wound forms within a relationship, it sets up a way of reading the world that makes whatever resembles the original climate feel familiar, and therefore attractive. It is also the ground on which emotional dependency takes root.

7. You cannot stay still

Filling every hour. A second job, a series always on in the background, the phone the moment there is a gap, sport to the point of exhaustion, sometimes alcohol or other substances. Rest is uncomfortable, even unbearable.

This is not ambition or energy. It is avoidance. Silence lets what was pushed aside come back up; constant activity keeps the lid on. It works for years — until a forced stop, an illness or a break-up brings everything back at once.

What these signs do not say

They do not say that you are broken, or that you will have to live with it. They say that a part of you is still running on information from a time that is over, and that it has never received the all-clear.

That is precisely what can be worked on. Not by erasing the past, but by allowing your nervous system to finally have the experience it is missing: that it is over, that you are no longer the person without resources you were back then, and that you can let go.

When not to wait

Seek help without delay if you are having suicidal thoughts, if you are putting yourself in danger, or if you are currently exposed to violence. Frequently asked questions

How many of these signs does it take to speak of trauma?

There is no numerical threshold, and this list is not a test. These signs can also have other causes, including medical ones. What matters is not the number but how persistent they are and how much they affect your daily life. Only a conversation with a professional can bring clarity.

Can you have these signs without remembering a specific event?

Yes, and it is common. This is typically the case with trauma built up through accumulation — repeated humiliation, an unpredictable parent, emotional loneliness in childhood — where no single event stands out. Having no striking memory in no way invalidates what you feel.

Can these reactions appear years later?

Yes. A traumatic memory can lie dormant for a long time and be reactivated by a life event: a birth, a bereavement, a separation, time off work, or simply the moment life becomes calm enough for what was held down to come back up.
